# The playable high-school mechanics path

Last reviewed: 4 September 2026

This document describes the route a learner can actually complete in the current application. It is narrower than the full curriculum map on purpose. A chapter appears here only when its prerequisite gate, instruction, interaction, feedback, evidence record, and next step are connected in the product.

The route uses one visual and conceptual anchor throughout: an idealized model of a 1995 manual BMW 525i sedan. Learner-declared quantities such as the teaching mass are model inputs, not claims about a measured car.

## The sequence

| Chapter | Physical question | Mathematics developed and used | Required evidence | Opens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Measure and model a wheel | How can a tire marking become a usable geometric model? | Ratios, millimetres and inches, circle geometry, RPM, graph comparison | Four entered calculations and a model-limit explanation | Complete-car motion |
| 2. Describe complete-car motion | How do words, signs, equations, and graphs describe one run? | Signed quantities, rates, linear equations, slope, graph reading | Four investigations with prediction, calculation, and graph evidence | Guided driving |
| 3. Control and stop the car | Can the learner apply the motion model while controlling the car? | Live position, velocity, acceleration, and a measured stopping interval | Reach the target speed and stop inside the assigned position window | Wheel inspection |
| 4. Connect road motion to wheel rotation | How does road speed become wheel angle, RPM, and revolutions? | Circumference, unit conversion, ratios, angular rate | Two wheel checkpoints and live telemetry | Path 02 |
| 5. Connect force to acceleration | What net force produces a measured acceleration? | Variables, direct proportion, multiplication, and newton units | Qualitative relationship, entered force, velocity-graph evidence | Ramp force components |
| 6. Resolve gravity on a ramp | How does ramp angle change uphill motion? | Signs, right-triangle trigonometry, components, equation evaluation | Direction prediction, entered acceleration magnitude, turning-point interpretation | Energy accounting |
| 7. Close an energy budget | Where does initial kinetic energy go during an ideal climb? | Squared speed, rearrangement, joules/kilojoules, proportional reasoning | Mass-independence prediction, entered kinetic energy, total-energy graph | Impulse design |
| 8. Design a longer stopping pulse | How can the same momentum change occur with lower average force? | Products, inverse proportion, force–time area, unit conversion | Stopping-time prediction, entered average force, impulse-area interpretation | Mechanics-route completion |

## Why this order

The sequence follows a stable dependency rather than arranging topics around whichever car part looks interesting. Learners first describe motion, then explain changes in motion through force, then use energy and momentum as complementary system models. The current [AP Physics 1 framework](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-physics-1) likewise places kinematics before force and translational dynamics, work/energy/power, and linear momentum. [OpenStax High School Physics](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/preface) supplies a broader school-level check on the same mechanics foundations.

The Next Generation Science Standards expect high-school learners to analyze data linking net force, mass, and acceleration and explicitly name ramps as a suitable one-dimensional context in [HS.Forces and Interactions](https://www.nextgenscience.org/topic-arrangement/hsforces-and-interactions). The [HS-PS3 energy evidence statements](https://www.nextgenscience.org/sites/default/files/HS%20PS3_Evidence%20Statements%20Jan%202015.pdf) call for an algebraic computational model, prediction, and identification of model limitations. Those expectations are why the app does not award progress for merely moving a slider.

## How mathematics and physics are combined

The course uses a just-in-time hybrid:

1. A vehicle question establishes the physical need.
2. The learner predicts a relationship before seeing the output.
3. A worked example demonstrates the mathematical structure with different values.
4. The learner calculates with the declared test values and requested unit.
5. A graph must support the same claim.
6. The conclusion and model inputs are saved to the notebook.

The [Physics Union Mathematics approach](https://pum.islephysics.net/approach.html) is an important precedent for moving from observation and qualitative explanation into quantitative models and multiple representations. The US Department of Education's [instruction and study practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1) supports interleaving worked examples with problems and connecting graphical, verbal, concrete, and abstract representations. These sources support the design choices; they do not prove that this particular app teaches effectively.

Mathematics and physics evidence remain separate in storage. That makes it possible to distinguish a sound physical prediction with weak algebra from a correct calculation with weak interpretation. The present feedback begins this distinction but is not yet a full diagnostic tutor.

## What completion means now

Completing a chapter means the learner produced the required in-app evidence for its current fixed structure. It does not mean accredited credit, psychometrically validated mastery, or readiness for real vehicle testing. Current thresholds and prompts are product hypotheses.

Before the route should be described as a finished course, it still needs:

- review by high-school physics teachers, a physicist, and a mathematics educator;
- observation with representative learners, including learners with prerequisite gaps;
- generated problem variants and delayed retrieval so answers cannot be memorized as a route;
- written explanations and transfer tasks scored with a transparent rubric;
- clearer misconception-specific remediation;
- keyboard-only, screen-reader, magnification, reduced-motion, and contrast evaluation;
- a documented revision cycle based on learning evidence, including negative findings.

The next content path should begin only after those reviews strengthen the present route. Path 03 is planned to connect rotational dynamics, traction, torque, gear ratios, and one constrained modification decision using the same 525i.
