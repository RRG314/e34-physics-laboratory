# Project overview

## The question behind the project

Physics courses often move from one clean example to the next: a block on a ramp, a point mass, a spring, a circuit, a sound wave. Those examples are useful, but the connections between them can disappear when the chapter ends.

E34 Physics Laboratory asks what happens when the examples belong to one persistent object. A car can move, rotate, transfer energy, heat its brakes, vibrate on its suspension, carry electrical current, move fluids, transmit sound, and collect measurements. The BMW E34 is old enough to be mechanically legible and complex enough to support years of increasingly sophisticated study.

The car is not meant to replace laboratory equipment or become a forced analogy for every topic. When another apparatus explains a concept more honestly, the course should use it and reconnect to the vehicle later.

## Who it is for

The intended audience includes:

- high-school learners studying their first formal physics course;
- university students moving into calculus-based mechanics and modeling;
- teachers looking for connected investigations and graph-rich examples;
- E34 owners who can contribute carefully documented measurements and observations;
- researchers studying simulation-supported learning, model use, or transfer.

The application should remain understandable to a learner who likes cars but has not taken physics, and useful to a learner who knows physics but has never worked on a car.

## The learning progression

The vehicle family provides a narrative for added complexity. It is a pedagogical progression, not a ranking of value or a claim that one model contains “more physics” than another.

| Stage | Vehicle context | Added learning demand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High school, year 1 | 518i | Measurement, motion, force, and energy in a deliberately simple system |
| High school, year 2 | 520i | Periodic processes, frequency, sound, and heat in an inline-six context |
| High school, year 3 | 525i | The complete algebra-based physics spine and the main laboratory vehicle |
| High school, year 4 | 525iX or Touring | Branches in all-wheel-drive topology, load placement, and model comparison |
| University, year 1 | 530i | Versioned evidence, fluid and thermal systems, and the importance of model year |
| University, year 2 | 535i | Torque, gearing, road load, braking work, and multivariable experiments |
| University, years 3–4 | 540i | Calculus-based coupled powertrain, thermal, and numerical models |
| Graduate design studio | 540i M-Sport | Suspension, braking, tire balance, optimization, and package-level tradeoffs |
| Research | M5 | Experiment design, parameter identification, uncertainty, and competing models |

The 525i remains the central high-school vehicle. The other models create branches and later contexts rather than replacing everything a learner already understands.

## What makes it a game

Progress comes from demonstrated capability:

`learn → predict → test → explain → certify → install → prove`

A learner earns access to a tool or modification by showing the physics needed to use it. A powerful part may still be a poor choice if the tires, brakes, cooling, driveline, or mathematical model cannot support it. More than one build can reach a similar result, allowing learners to compare the different physics and tradeoffs involved.

The project avoids generic experience points. The reward is a new physical action, instrument, vehicle system, experiment, or modeling choice.

## Boundaries

This is an educational research prototype. It is not affiliated with BMW, is not a repair manual, and must not be used to approve a real modification or safety-critical design. Virtual impacts and drops exist because they are unsafe and unsuitable as real learner experiments.
