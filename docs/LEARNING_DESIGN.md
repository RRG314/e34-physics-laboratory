# Learning design

## A course spine, not a collection of activities

The project begins with a year of high-school physics and then adds advanced high-school, university, and research depth. It does not claim that one published syllabus is best for every learner. The sequence is a documented synthesis of established curriculum frameworks, open courses, and learning research.

[OpenStax High School Physics](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/preface) supplies the broad high-school chapter spine. [Next Generation Science Standards](https://www.nextgenscience.org/topic-arrangement/hsforces-and-interactions) contribute performance expectations such as interpreting motion graphs, studying ramps, conserving momentum, and designing protective devices. [AP Physics 1](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-physics-1) and [AP Physics 2](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-physics-2) extend the high-school pathway. [MIT OpenCourseWare](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/syllabus/) provides a reference for calculus-based mechanics and later university work.

The supporting mathematics follows the high-school categories in the [Common Core State Standards for Mathematics](https://corestandards.org/mathematics-standards/) and the modeling emphasis of [AP Precalculus](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-precalculus/about-ap-precalculus) and [AP Calculus](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-calculus-ab).

## Course sequence

| Module | Main physical ideas | Vehicle context |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Measurement, units, precision, and uncertainty | Wheel, wheelbase, time, and mass measurements |
| 2 | Position, displacement, velocity, acceleration, and graphs | Instrumented straight-line run |
| 3 | Forces and Newton's laws | Contact patches, towing, and ramps |
| 4 | Circular and rotational motion | Wheels, gearing, driveshaft, and differential |
| 5 | Momentum and impulse | Low-speed virtual barrier and restraint pulse |
| 6 | Work, energy, and power | Ramps, braking, and energy accounting |
| 7 | Thermal physics | Brakes, coolant, radiator, and tires |
| 8 | Waves, sound, and vibration | Suspension response, imbalance, and engine order |
| 9 | Electricity and magnetism | Battery, starter, alternator, lamps, sensors, and grounds |
| 10 | Integrated advanced high-school physics | Choosing and testing a model for a complete maneuver |
| 11 | Calculus-based vehicle dynamics | Continuous-time, coupled, and numerical models |
| 12 | Research practice | Identifiability, uncertainty, validation, and falsification |

The opening motion sequence, one scaffolded wheel-and-tire mathematics investigation, and a set of proving-ground models are playable today. They do not yet satisfy this entire course plan. The table defines the intended sequence, not a claim of completed content.

## What every lesson must contain

Before a lesson is accepted, it needs:

1. a clear prerequisite and a brief prior-knowledge check;
2. one observable outcome and a visible success criterion;
3. a physical question or phenomenon before formal equations;
4. coordinated words, diagrams, graphs, equations, units, and animation;
5. a worked example followed by a problem with less support;
6. a prediction made before the experiment runs;
7. feedback connected to a known misconception;
8. an assessment in a changed context, not merely a repeated calculation;
9. a later retrieval opportunity before dependent work.

This pattern reflects Carnegie Mellon's guidance on [aligning objectives, activities, and assessments](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/assessment/basics/alignment.html), as well as the US Department of Education's recommendations to space learning, interleave worked examples and problems, connect concrete and abstract representations, and ask explanatory questions in [Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1).

## Modeling and active learning

The learner should encounter behavior, make a prediction, and test a model before being told that a formula is the answer. This draws from [Modeling Instruction](https://www.modelinginstruction.org/effective/), the research approach behind [PhET Interactive Simulations](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/research), and the broader evidence for [active learning in STEM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4060654/).

The interface also follows the main principles of [CAST's Universal Design for Learning](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/): offer more than one way to engage, represent an idea, and demonstrate understanding while keeping the intellectual goal intact.

## Mathematics and feedback

Mathematics has its own prerequisite graph because a sign error, an algebra error, and a physics misconception need different responses. Early modules use ratios, unit conversion, signed quantities, algebra, geometry, slope, and graph area. Later modules add trigonometry, functions, calculus, differential equations, linear algebra, statistics, numerical methods, and uncertainty propagation.

Correct answers are evidence, not proof of permanent mastery. Current thresholds are design hypotheses. They must be tested with learners, and a future adaptive system should report uncertainty rather than presenting a score as a diagnosis.

## Evaluation

The educational approach is evidence-informed; the application itself is not yet a proven intervention. Evaluation should include expert review, usability work with learners at different levels of mathematical readiness, concept and transfer measures, delayed retention, accessibility testing, and analysis of where progression gates help or frustrate learning. Completion time and click-through rate are not sufficient educational outcomes.
