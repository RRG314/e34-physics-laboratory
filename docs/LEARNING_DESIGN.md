# Learning design

## One car, recurring domains, increasing depth

E34 Physics Laboratory follows one 525i from high-school foundations toward research practice. The vehicle remains stable while the mathematics, physical models, experimental independence, and evidence quality become more demanding.

The full rationale, source comparison, curriculum hierarchy, domain map, and modification gates are in the [integrated mathematics and physics path](INTEGRATED_MATH_PHYSICS_PATH.md). This document states the working rules used to accept or reject lessons.

The project does not claim that one syllabus is best for every learner. Its sequence is a documented synthesis of established programs and evidence: [Physics Union Mathematics](https://pum.islephysics.net/about.html) provides a close precedent for developing mathematics through physics in grades 6–12; [OpenStax Physics](https://openstax.org/books/physics/pages/preface), [AP Physics](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-physics-1), and [NGSS](https://www.nextgenscience.org/practice/using-mathematics-and-computational-thinking) establish recognizable high-school scope and practices; [MIT OpenCourseWare](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/syllabus/) informs calculus-based and later university depth.

## Curriculum structure

Ten domain families recur at five depths:

| Domain family | Principal 525i systems |
| --- | --- |
| Measurement, data, and modeling | Whole vehicle, tools, and sensors |
| Motion | Body, wheels, speedometer, and odometer |
| Forces, traction, and equilibrium | Tires, contact patches, ramps, and steering |
| Energy, momentum, and safety | Brakes, powertrain, ramps, and virtual restraint models |
| Rotation and powertrain | Wheels, gears, clutch, driveshaft, and differential |
| Oscillations, waves, and sound | Springs, dampers, tires, engine vibration, and cabin |
| Thermal systems and fluids | Brakes, coolant, radiator, oil, tires, and fuel tank |
| Electricity, magnetism, and sensing | Battery, grounds, starter, alternator, lights, and sensors |
| Light, instrumentation, and materials | Headlamps, mirrors, displays, body, and restraint materials |
| Integrated engineering | Whole-vehicle experiments, compatibility, and validation |

Each family has a high-school foundation, advanced-high-school, first-year-college, advanced-college, and research treatment. A treatment is a deliberately authored lesson sequence—not one generic activity with harder numbers.

The opening motion sequence, one scaffolded wheel-and-tire mathematics investigation, and several proving-ground models are playable today. They do not yet satisfy the whole map. Planned treatments are shown as planned and award no credit.

## Mathematics and physics belong together, but remain distinguishable

Prerequisite mathematics comes first when it would otherwise prevent the learner from engaging with the physical problem. New mathematics is normally introduced when a physical observation gives it purpose, followed by a short mathematics studio and a return to the vehicle investigation.

The working loop is:

`check readiness → observe → represent → build mathematics → predict → test → explain → transfer → retrieve later`

Mathematics and physics have separate goals and evidence. The application must be able to distinguish at least these cases:

- the physical idea is sound but arithmetic, algebra, or notation failed;
- a procedure was followed correctly but the physical interpretation is missing;
- the representation was misread;
- the learner chose an unsuitable model;
- the model is suitable but the evidence is not precise enough to decide.

The mathematics path begins with arithmetic, fractions, ratios, units, signed quantities, geometry, graphs, and basic statistics. It proceeds through algebra and functions, trigonometry, exponentials, logarithms, regression, calculus, differential equations, linear algebra, probability, numerical methods, uncertainty, estimation, and optimization.

## Lesson acceptance checklist

A lesson is not ready merely because its simulation runs. Before it is accepted, it needs:

1. one observable learning outcome and a visible success criterion;
2. explicit mathematics and physics prerequisites with a brief readiness check;
3. a physical question, measurement, or phenomenon before formal equations;
4. coordinated words, diagrams, tables, graphs, equations, units, and animation where appropriate;
5. a worked example, an incorrect or contrasting example, and progressively less-supported practice;
6. a qualitative and, when appropriate, quantitative prediction before the experiment runs;
7. controlled variables, recorded evidence, and visible model assumptions;
8. feedback connected to a known error or misconception;
9. an explanation and assessment in a changed context;
10. a later retrieval opportunity before dependent work.

This pattern follows the observation-to-application cycle described by [Physics Union Mathematics](https://pum.islephysics.net/approach.html) and the recommendations on spacing, worked examples, connected representations, and explanatory questions in the US Department of Education's [Organizing Instruction and Study to Improve Student Learning](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/1).

## Progression and adaptation

Progress is based on evidence, not time spent or a generic experience score. A learner may receive a refresher, prerequisite studio, additional representation, or changed problem based on the specific gap. The system should not silently change the success criterion after an attempt.

An advanced learner is asked to make more decisions: select a model, identify necessary measurements, design a controlled test, compare residuals, and defend limitations. A beginning learner receives more structure, but the physical claim remains real.

Correct answers are evidence, not proof of permanent mastery. Current thresholds are design hypotheses. They must be calibrated with learners, and a future adaptive system should report uncertainty rather than presenting a score as a diagnosis.

## Modifications are capstones

A vehicle modification is available only after the learner can predict its intended effect and identify the systems it may disturb. Installing it is not the reward by itself. The learner must test the change, compare it with a baseline, account for compatibility, and explain the result.

This allows different solutions to reach a similar goal through different physical tradeoffs. Tires, brakes, gearing, suspension, cooling, and sensors become meaningful branches after their prerequisite domains—not substitutes for the core course.

## Modeling and active learning

The learner should encounter behavior, make a prediction, and test a model before being told that a formula is the answer. The decision to make learners actively predict, calculate, measure, and explain is consistent with the broader evidence for [active learning in STEM](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1319030111).

The car is not forced into every topic. When another apparatus explains a concept more honestly, the course should use it and reconnect to vehicle measurement or instrumentation only where that link is genuine.

## Evaluation

The design is evidence-informed; the application is not yet a proven educational intervention. Evaluation must include expert review, work with learners at different mathematical starting points, explanation and transfer measures, delayed retention, accessibility testing, and analysis of progression decisions. Completion time, clicks, and correct final answers are not sufficient outcomes.
